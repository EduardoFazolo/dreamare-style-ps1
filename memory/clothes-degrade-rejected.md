Copying the faces' PS1 treatment onto the garments was rejected (Hushford test, 2026-10-26). The test shrank the photo textures to ~24 px and cut the clothes into flat 7 cm facets. User: "the clothes look worse and even less artistic actually". The code was removed.

**Why:** degrading a photo garment does not make it hand-made. It only loses the detail; nothing is authored. What the user liked on the v2 faces was that they "feel a little more hand created".

**How to apply:** "artistic clothes" must add authored design (silhouette, painted folds/seams, drawn patterns per [[artist-method]]), not subtract resolution or geometry. Show the user one direction as stills before building anything. Related: [[hushford-video]], [[expressive-clear-faces]].

**2026-10-26, second rejection (art/clothes/concepts-v1.png):** three concepts were cut from Met museum garment photos: an 18th-century quilted petticoat robe, a smock with a shawl, and a bed jacket with a bonnet. User: "too weird, like what period even is this supposed to be?" I had let the museum search results drive the design, so the outfits mixed 1700s-1800s costume with no period at all. **Fix:** decide the period and archetype first (our faces are modern photos; the look is PS1/PS2-era everyday clothes), then find real CC0 textures for that period. Never design from whatever the reference archive happens to hold.
